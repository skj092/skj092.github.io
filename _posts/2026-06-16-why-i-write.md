I have been writing in a private journal since 2021 — five years of entries, late nights, train commutes, tent camps in the Himalayas, bad weeks, good weeks, and everything in between. Somewhere in there I described writing as *"like sweeping my mind — it gives me freshness."* I still believe that. I probably always will.

So why start a public blog now?

The honest answer is that the journals have become too comfortable. Writing only for yourself is safe. Nobody can tell you the thinking is muddled, the logic is wrong, the conclusion obvious. The page agrees with everything you say. That is useful — I have worked through a lot of pain and confusion in those private notes — but it stops being enough when you actually want to develop ideas.

There is a sharpening that happens when you know someone might read what you write. You can't leave a half-formed thought and promise yourself you'll come back to it. You have to finish the argument. That finishing is where the real thinking lives.

---

I also want a record that I was paying attention.

I am thirty years old. I came from Bihar fifteen years ago with a vague idea that I wanted to build things with code. Since then I have become an ML engineer, read roughly sixty books, trekked at 14,500 feet in the Himalayas, trained in MMA, moved my family into a better home, and survived several iterations of myself — the anxious early-career version, the lonely Bengaluru version, the grinding competitive-ML version. Each version had something worth keeping.

I don't want all of that to just disappear into my own head. Writing it down — publicly, with care — feels like the right form of respect for the years.

---

What will I write about here?

Mostly what I am already thinking about: machine learning (building it, understanding it, reading about it), books (what I am reading and what it does to me), training (the body as a project), and the general question of how to live a focused life when the world wants your attention scattered across a hundred feeds.

I have no intention of being comprehensive or authoritative. I am not an authority on anything. I am a person who finds things interesting and wants to think out loud about them.

I will write when I have something to say. I will try not to write when I don't.

That is the whole plan.
